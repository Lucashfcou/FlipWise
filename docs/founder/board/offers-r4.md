# Offers lens (round 4)
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

Lens: value equation, market selection, offer stack (published framework). I score the business as it stands, not the founder.

## Does the vote change vs round 3 (score 4)? Why
Vote stays FUND IF. Score moves 4 to 5.

Up:
- Dream outcome is now concrete and broad. "Know what to pay and what it will resell for" now covers 247 products in 10 categories, not 35 items with example prices.
- Effort is lower. The eBay.es data is real, cleaned, and shown as a single decision (Buy/Negotiate/Pass) plus a max offer.
- Honesty about thin data (products left blank instead of a doubtful number) helps perceived likelihood.

Not up:
- Likelihood is still unproven. Prices are asked prices from one marketplace (eBay.es), not sold prices, and the buyer sells on Vinted/OLX. Nobody has checked that the number matches what a reseller actually gets.
- Zero users besides the founder, zero registered sales, zero willingness-to-pay evidence.
- The paid tier cannot be sold yet (no accounts, server, payments, hosting). The commercial terms of the eBay API for a paid product are unchecked.

Market check: pain is real for active resellers. Money to spend is unknown. Reachability is plausible (reseller communities) but untested. Market size in Europe and share willing to pay: open questions.

## Is there a future in the paid plan? The hook, and the main risk
Yes, a conditional one.

Hook: "Never overpay for an item that is not in the catalogue, in seconds, from a screenshot." Pain moment: the reseller stands at a fair or reads a listing, the product is not covered or the price is unclear, and a wrong buy costs real money. Print reading plus AI search for any product turns the app from a lookup table into a tool that answers every time. That is the paid value, because it removes the "no data" dead end.

Main risk: the paid hook is the least proven part. Print reading was never measured on real screenshots, and the AI-search price is flagged "unreliable". If the headline paid feature gives low-confidence numbers, the buyer pays for a guess, and trust in the free tier suffers too. Second risk: the base number is asked prices from eBay.es. If it is off versus Vinted/OLX sale prices, no paid feature fixes that. Third: dependence on eBay API terms for commercial use (unchecked).

Value equation for the paid tier today: outcome high, likelihood low (no proof), time low (seconds), effort low. Likelihood is the weak term. Fix it with proof, not with features.

## Free vs paid: what to put where, and what is missing from the paid tier
Free (the proof and the habit):
- Large catalogue with prices, single-item evaluation, basic stock, lots, basic tax view.
- Keep the evaluation free. It is what earns trust and word of mouth. Do not gate the core decision.

Paid (the "where it saves or earns money" layer):
- Print reading and AI search outside the catalogue (the hook).
- Alerts on products the user watches (move up: it brings the user back daily and ties to money).
- Cross-country eBay price comparison (arbitrage across markets is a real reseller edge).
- Extra categories, exports, history.

Missing from the paid tier:
1. A result tied to the buyer's money: a "profit you captured" or "overpay avoided" tally, from the "did the app get it right?" log. It is the proof of ROI that justifies a subscription and that guarantee wording can rest on.
2. A guarantee. Candidate: a trial that ends with a plain report, "the app flagged X items, you bought Y, estimated margin Z". If it saves nothing, cancel with no charge. A guarantee is only affordable once cost per user is known.
3. Real sold-price data from the users themselves, shown as a benefit (own sales improve the numbers). This is the only route to the sold-price truth that eBay does not give.
4. Pricing model choice is open: flat monthly versus per-lookup. Per-lookup matches the real cost of AI calls and the irregular use of part-time resellers; flat matches the habit of daily users. Test both. No market figures asserted here; price points are hypotheses.

Consider cutting from paid: generic exports and "more categories" as headline features. They do not answer "why pay today".

## The 3 defects that remain
1. No evidence the number is right. Asked prices from one marketplace, no sold prices, no comparison against what resellers really get on Vinted/OLX. The whole offer rests on this.
2. No users and no willingness-to-pay signal. Everything, including the plan, comes from the founder's own use.
3. The paid hook is unmeasured and unbuildable as-is. Print reading has no quality data, and there are no accounts, server, payments, or hosting. API commercial terms are also unchecked.

## Next 3 actions, in order
1. Check eBay API terms for a paid commercial product. Cheap, and it can kill the business model. Do first.
2. Measure the number: compare Flipfind's predicted price against the founder's real sold prices (and any friends') on 30+ items. Run print reading on 50+ real screenshots and record the hit rate.
3. Put the free version in front of 10-20 real resellers (public link, minimal hosting, no accounts) and watch usage. Then run a pre-sale or waitlist with a real price for the paid tier.

Build accounts and payments only after step 3 shows a signal.

## First thing to validate with real users, and the continue/stop criterion
First: do resellers who are not the founder use the free evaluation on real purchase decisions, and does the number match what they sell for?

Proposed thresholds (hypotheses to adjust before starting, not market facts):
- Continue: at least a set share (suggest roughly 30%) of invited resellers evaluate 5+ items in 2 weeks, the prediction is judged close to sold price in about 7 of 10 cases (the founder's own bar), and a meaningful number of them accept a real-price pre-sale or paid trial for the print/AI tier. Fix the target count in advance.
- Stop or rethink: people try it once and leave, the number is often wrong against sold prices, or nobody commits money when asked. Then the data, not the features, is the problem.

Decide the thresholds in writing before collecting data, to avoid bending them afterwards.

## Conditions
- Eliminate the eBay commercial-terms risk first.
- Show, on real sales, that the price tracks what resellers actually get.
- Get paid-intent evidence from non-founder resellers (pre-sale or priced waitlist) before building payments.
- Make print reading and AI search measurable (hit rate, confidence shown) before selling them as the hook.
- Open questions to name, not assume: market size in Europe, share willing to pay, acquisition cost, real cost per user for AI calls, right price and model (monthly vs per-lookup).
