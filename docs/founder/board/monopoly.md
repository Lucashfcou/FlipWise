# Monopoly lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- Real niche with a plausible small market to own first: Portuguese resellers of used consoles, controllers and PS4 games who buy on Vinted, OLX.pt and Wallapop. Small enough that a big player is unlikely to care early.
- The proprietary data idea is the only route to durability. Users' recorded real sales (model, condition, price paid, platform) could become a dataset that nobody else holds for Portuguese used electronics. That is a data network effect, if it reaches scale.
- Founder is a user of the product. That is a candidate "secret": he knows how the buy/sell margin decision is made in this niche. It is not yet a proven secret.
- Narrow MVP scope (consoles, accessories, about 100 PS4 games) fits the "start small, dominate" principle.
- Low cost to start, no dependency on breaking site terms. That avoids legal risk, though it costs data (see below).

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No data advantage at launch, and the data layers are all public or rented.** Affiliate feeds, Back Market, CeX, eBay API and PriceCharting are available to anyone. The only unique layer (user prints and recorded sales) does not exist on day one and depends on users who do not yet exist. A copycat in month six, or a reseller with a spreadsheet and CeX, matches the day-one product. Today the "10x better" claim is unproven: nothing shows this beats a manual check of recent Vinted/OLX listings.
2. **Distribution is not designed.** The brief names no way to reach the first 100 customers and no cost per customer. The market size is unknown. Resellers are scattered individuals, a hard group to reach, and a subscription of 5-15 EUR/month needs them to pay for something they now do free by browsing. Without a channel, the data flywheel never starts.
3. **Dependence on platforms that are also the substitute.** Vinted and OLX hold the real price data and could add their own price hints or sold-price display at any time. Also, the no-scraping choice means each evaluation needs manual user input (print or typed price), which adds effort and caps how much better than the free alternative the product can be.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- How many resellers in Portugal buy and sell used electronics often enough to pay monthly? Where does that number come from?
- What is the secret you know that other resellers and existing tools do not? State it in one sentence.
- Name the 100 first customers. Which communities, groups or fairs are they in, and what does each cost to reach?
- What does the app give that a user cannot get in two minutes by searching sold listings and CeX buy prices? Is it 10x better on speed, accuracy or both?
- Does the eBay API give usable sold data for these items? Does PriceCharting cover European prices at a viable cost? What happens to the product if both answer no?
- How many recorded sales are needed before your own dataset beats the public sources? How long to reach that number at your expected user count?
- What stops a competitor (or Vinted itself) copying this in month six?
- Will the data stay yours? Do users agree to contribute their sales anonymously, and is that compliant with the data rules you work under?
- If this stays limited to PS4 and consoles, is the market large enough to live on, and what is the second market you expand into?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Fund only a small, time-boxed test, not a build-out, and only if:
1. The 2-4 week founder test passes the stated bar (7 of 10 suggestions near achieved sale price), AND it beats the manual method for speed or accuracy by a margin the founder can state in numbers.
2. A distribution test shows a path to customers: for example 50 to 100 target resellers reached through named channels, with a measured cost and at least some paying or pre-paying (a real price, not interest).
3. The data-source questions are answered: eBay sold data access and PriceCharting European coverage confirmed or replaced by a working alternative.
4. A written plan shows how the user-sales dataset becomes the moat: an incentive for users to record sales, a target volume, and an estimate of time to the point where it beats public sources.
5. The founder can name the market size for Portugal (even a rough, sourced estimate) and the next market to expand into.

If conditions 2 and 4 cannot be met, the vote becomes PASS: it would be a useful personal tool, not a defensible business.
