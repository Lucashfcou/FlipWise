# Monopoly lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

Framework applied: Zero to One (small market, distribution, durability, the secret). Business scored, not the founder.

## What is strong
- **Small, definable starting market.** Individual resellers of used consoles, controllers and PS4 games in Portugal who buy at fairs, Vinted, OLX.pt and Wallapop and sell on Vinted and OLX.pt. Narrow enough to dominate if reachable. Its size is an open question.
- **A real secret candidate.** Sold-price data for used electronics in Portugal. Vinted and OLX show asking prices, not what items sold for. If the app captures real sales, it holds data nobody else has.
- **Buyer-side focus.** The brief's sources show new prices, refurbished prices (Back Market, Rebuy) or buy-back floors (CeX). None is named as answering "the most I should pay for this listing, for my margin". Not verified by a competitor scan.
- **Data compounds.** Each logged sale improves the next user's price range. A data network effect, weak at first, and the only durable moat on the table.
- **Switching cost.** Cost, days held and profit per item become the user's books. Leaving means losing that history.
- **Domain insight.** The founder lives the buy/sell workflow.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No distribution.** The brief has no channel for the first 100 customers, no acquisition cost, no market size. Resellers are a scattered niche in a small country. If the answer is paid ads, a 5 to 15 EUR/month price cannot cover them.
2. **The secret does not exist yet, and a copycat is cheap.** v0.1 ships with example prices, not real ones. With no scraping, the only proprietary data is what users log, and users will not log sales into an app with no useful prices. Cold-start deadlock. A margin calculator can be rebuilt in a weekend; only accumulated sold-price data is hard to copy, and there is none.
3. **Platform and category dependence.** Buying and selling happen on Vinted and OLX, which could add price hints or restrict the workflow. PS4 is an ageing category. Ten-year durability for a single-category tool is doubtful. Open: does a reseller of a few items a month earn enough to pay 5 to 15 EUR?

## What is missing from the product (max 5 items, by impact, plus what to cut)
Answer to "what makes it a daily, paid tool": the gap is real data and a loop that grows it, not another screen.
1. **Real price data in the engine.** Replace example prices with legitimate sources (eBay if sold data is approved, CeX, PriceCharting) plus the founder's own purchases and sales. Nothing else matters without it.
2. **Frictionless capture.** Print-to-listing reading with user confirmation, and one-tap "sold at X" in Stock. This feeds the sold-price dataset, the only moat.
3. **Shared, anonymous sold-price layer** shown with sample size and confidence. Where the network effect starts. Needs a backend and user consent.
4. **Alerts from user-entered watches** (target item, max price), no scraping. Gives a reason to open the app daily.
5. **Listing draft generator** with suggested sell price. Closes buy-to-sell and keeps the user in the app.

**Cut or delay:** sneakers, phones, notebooks, accounts, payments, price-history charts. Stay on consoles, controllers and PS4 games until one category has real depth.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- How many people in Portugal resell at a scale where 5 to 15 EUR/month is worth it? What is the source?
- Where do the first 100 paying users come from, and what does each cost? Which groups, forums or fairs already gather them?
- What stops a copycat in month six? What do you hold that they cannot get?
- Where do the first 500 real sold prices come from?
- Does eBay's API return sold data for your categories, and does it cover Portugal?
- What is 10 times better than a spreadsheet plus a Vinted search?
- What stops Vinted or OLX adding price hints themselves?
- Does the margin scheme for second-hand goods change what the app must record?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
- **Prove distribution first.** 20 resellers use it for 4 weeks and 5 pay or pre-commit, reached through a named channel at near-zero cost. If not reachable, stop.
- **Replace example prices** with real data from at least two legitimate sources for the MVP categories, and pass the brief's own test (7 in 10 close to the real sale) on real purchases.
- **Show the data loop works:** users log sold prices without being pushed, and ranges improve with sample size. Set a numeric gate in advance (the number is a judgment call, not a fact).
- **Own one niche first:** one category, one country, one platform pair, until the app is the best answer there.
- **Size the market** with an actual count of resellers (groups, sellers with repeated sales) before spending anything beyond the founder's time.
